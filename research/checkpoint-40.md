# Typography-Dominant Cover Corpus: Checkpoint 40

This checkpoint records forty album or release covers that were directly screened as typography-dominant candidates. It is a research milestone, not a final corpus and not a list of acclaimed music.

## What passed

- 40 cover-design candidates, each backed by a cover-specific museum, award, exhibition, or recognized design-history source
- 36 direct `visual-pass` decisions and 4 explicit `visual-second-pass` decisions
- 28 T5 covers, where a wordmark, title, numeral, or glyph can organize and identify the cover without a separate image
- 12 T4 covers, where lettering is the strongest organizing mass but still interacts with imagery
- 7 region labels and more than 10 genre labels
- all four target eras represented
- 9 candidates using non-Latin, mixed-script, encoded, or custom-glyph systems
- a named design credit and a work-specific visual observation for every candidate

Third-party covers were inspected from live source pages and temporary contact sheets outside the repository. No third-party cover image is stored here.

## What this checkpoint disproved

- A famous or museum-held album is not necessarily useful here. Image-dominant covers remain below T4 even when the overall package is acclaimed.
- Package awards do not prove that the front cover is typography-dominant.
- Typography dominance is not the same as large readable text. Encoded marks, material relief, cropping, repetition, and unfamiliar alphabets can carry the cover.
- A source-rich jazz or European cluster cannot stand in for global design history.

## Required correction before checkpoint 80

The next forty candidates must not preserve the current distribution. Research should prioritize:

1. 1980–1999 and 2015–present.
2. East Asia, Southeast Asia, Middle East and North Africa, Sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America, the Caribbean, and Oceania.
3. Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, and additional South Asian scripts, plus genuinely mixed-script systems.
4. Hip-hop, metal, classical, ambient, club, reggae/dub, regional pop, and experimental releases outside the existing jazz/electronic clusters.
5. Independent designer, label, and regional archives that can reduce dependence on Cooper Hewitt and Best Art Vinyl.

No candidate becomes `evidence-complete` until acclaim evidence, front-cover visual evidence, and design-credit evidence are recorded as separable sources. Only `evidence-complete` works may enter the final `research/corpus.yaml`.
