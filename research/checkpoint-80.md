# Typography-Dominant Cover Corpus: Checkpoint 80

This checkpoint records eighty album or release covers directly screened as typography-dominant candidates. It remains a research milestone, not a final corpus and not a list of acclaimed music.

## What passed

- 80 cover-design candidates, each backed by a cover-specific museum, award, exhibition, design-research, or recognized design-history source
- 74 direct `visual-pass` decisions and 6 explicit `visual-second-pass` decisions
- 44 T5 covers, where a wordmark, title, numeral, or glyph can organize and identify the cover without a separate image
- 36 T4 covers, where lettering is the strongest organizing mass but still interacts with imagery
- 12 region labels and 58 genre labels
- 34 candidates using non-Latin, mixed-script, encoded, Braille, numeral, or custom-glyph systems
- a named design credit and a work-specific visual observation for every candidate

Third-party covers were inspected from live source pages and temporary contact sheets outside the repository. No third-party cover image is stored here.

The 80-work gate is deliberately not the final distribution. It proves that the research method can add non-Latin, mixed-script, and non-Western work without relaxing the T4/T5 visual threshold. It also makes the remaining bias measurable.

## What this checkpoint established

- Typography can be a figure, an object, a material surface, a ritual field, a data system, or a multilingual translation layer; it is not limited to a font choice over an image.
- Mixed scripts work best when they share one construction logic—signage, stamp, poster, calligraphy, or grid—rather than when one script is used as decorative flavor.
- Package awards are useful discovery sources but do not remove the need to inspect the square front cover separately.
- Contemporary European award archives are efficient for visual screening but are not a substitute for a global final corpus.
