# Bounded verified typography principles

This is the current teaching set for the skill. It is deliberately bounded: it
turns already verified research into decisions that can improve a cover run.
It is not a genre catalogue, a style menu, or a reason to research more covers
during production. Do not copy the named works; use the construction rule.

## How to use this reference

First identify the visual problem in the brief. Then use at most one primary
principle and one supporting principle. A principle is useful only if it changes
the direction's composition, lettering specification, or evaluation criterion.
If none applies, use the normal pattern and typography references instead.

Do not add a research task when a cover run does not match a card. Record the
unmet need in the run ledger; research can be reopened only after repeated,
documented production failures show a real gap.

## 1. Encode a title as a learnable sign system

**Verified precedents:** David Bowie, *Blackstar*; Joy Division, *Substance*.

Use this when a short title needs to become the cover's primary identity rather
than a caption. Define the minimum character cues that must survive, then apply
one repeatable substitution, module, or glyph rule. Make a one-color proof
before adding texture or imagery.

**Reject when:** the listener would need an explanation to read the title at
128 px, or every letter receives a different decorative treatment.

## 2. Make deliberate under-design carry the entire square

**Verified precedents:** Sex Pistols, *Never Mind the Bollocks*; Charli xcx,
*BRAT*.

Use this when cultural friction, bluntness, or anti-polish is part of the
release. Reduce the field to one dominant text mass and a controlled defect:
blur, crude registration, cut-out collision, or deliberately cheap surface.
The defect must be specific enough to look authored rather than merely faulty.

**Reject when:** the result is only a generic flat color plus ordinary type, or
the title's poor legibility is being mistaken for attitude.

## 3. Treat the title as physical evidence, not an overlay

**Verified precedents:** Terence Lam, *Camouflage*; Legião Urbana, *Dois*.

Use this when the song has pressure, absence, camouflage, fragility, or a
physical constraint. Let the title be molded, engraved, debossed, transparent,
or revealed by light. Define what makes it visible at a thumbnail size: edge
light, shadow, contrast boundary, or a secondary exact post-typeset lockup.

**Reject when:** material realism makes the title disappear without an intended
reading route.

## 4. Give multilingual type a governing reading route

**Verified precedents:** Muddy Basin Ramblers, *Formosa Medicine Show*;
*Hold That Tiger*; *Jug Band Millionaire*; Various artists, *Manshaa - Noise
Record*.

Use this for mixed-script releases or when the title needs a cultural frame.
Choose which script leads, define a reading route, and make the second script a
counterweight, border, stamp, or badge. Match optical energy rather than forcing
identical metrics. A title field can be dense only if one entry point remains
unmistakable at 128 px.

**Reject when:** non-Latin scripts are reduced to exotic decoration, or the
viewer cannot find the approved title string.

## 5. Let degradation preserve rhythm rather than destroy it

**Verified precedent:** Underworld, *Dubnobasswithmyheadman*.

Use controlled faxing, stretching, offset, scan noise, or copy artifacts only
when they represent signal loss, speed, collision, or mechanical pressure in
the track. Start with a clean, readable wordmark and transform it through one
specified process. Preserve at least one stable edge, baseline, or repeated
stroke rhythm.

**Reject when:** distortion is applied independently to each letter or becomes
an interchangeable grunge effect.

## 6. Make political or narrative language a visual mass

**Verified precedents:** Fela Kuti, *No Agreement*; Consolidated, *Business of
Punishment*.

Use a text field when phrases, warnings, or a statement are part of the song's
actual tension. Establish one title entry point, then define whether supporting
language behaves as a headline chorus, a field of evidence, or an institutional
block. Keep every non-title word out unless the user explicitly approves it.

**Reject when:** invented slogans or fake metadata create an unapproved second
message.

## 7. Make registration and layering part of the title's meaning

**Verified precedent:** Alagoas, *Alagoas*.

Use this when a release contains duplication, interference, competing selves,
or hidden alignment. Separate a title into a small number of color or material
layers, define the reveal condition, and test both aligned and misregistered
states. The title must remain a designed form even when the reveal is absent.

**Reject when:** the effect is just a decorative chromatic aberration with no
relationship to the song or title skeleton.

## Evidence boundary

The works above are cited by their candidate IDs in
`research/typographic-candidates.yaml`; the repository contains no cover-image
files. These cards are not evidence that a new artist or genre should look like
any precedent. They are narrowly stated construction rules to test against the
current release brief.
