## How to use the recipes

These are exploratory mechanisms, not tested success-rate claims or a menu
to apply in order. Start with a reading of the supplied music, then choose a
mechanism that makes it visible. Use one decisive operation per concept.
Do not combine all recipes in one image or turn each into the same centered
three-dimensional word with a figure beside it.

The exact title remains image-native and structurally decisive. It can occupy
positive mass, negative space, a plane, a trace, or a distributed reading
route. Its importance does not require maximum size. At production, it still
must read at 128 px and the cover must hold its main signal at 56 px.

## Optical and spatial readings

| ID | Mechanism | Meaning it can enact | Construct the difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| O01 | Figure/ground reversal | Belonging versus exclusion; presence made from absence | Make the title's counters and the surrounding silhouette exchange roles. Use a flat two-color field, not a word on a backdrop. |
| O02 | Anamorphic alignment | Almost arriving; a truth available from one position | Distribute title fragments across planes that align from the chosen camera. The one final view must read correctly. |
| O03 | Impossible return path | A loop that cannot restore the past | Let a continuous title stroke become a route whose one spatial join is impossible. Keep the impossibility large enough to inspect. |
| O04 | Scale reversal | A small responsibility becoming overwhelming | Make a letter detail operate at landscape scale, with one small contextual cue; avoid the standard full word monument plus hero. |
| O05 | Shadow as the true title | A hidden self; actions leaving another meaning | Ordinary non-letter objects cast one exact title-shaped shadow. Give the objects and shadow different jobs. |
| O06 | Reflection reveals the missing part | Public versus private; incomplete knowledge | A reflection completes a broken reading route. Specify orientation and which strokes belong to the object and reflection. |
| O07 | Ambiguous contour | Two relationships that cannot be held at once | One contour supports a title and a second simple image. Avoid tiny encoded details; both readings need visible shape evidence. |
| O08 | Near/far hierarchy reversal | Intimacy versus distance; memory as aggregation | A strong title reads at thumbnail scale while larger inspection reveals its meaningful components. The large-scale reading must not rely on fine noise. |

## Graphic force and restraint

Use `graphic-field` when positive/negative shape, edge, rhythm, or a two-color
rule creates the image. A flat cover does not need a simulated object,
physical light source, character, photographic perspective, or material patina.

| ID | Mechanism | Meaning it can enact | Construct the difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G01 | Two masses in collision | Mutual desire and resistance | Two flat shapes compress the exact title into their shared boundary; protect the reading route. |
| G02 | Monumental absence | A departure that reorganizes everything left | Most of the square is empty; one decisive cut or title-shaped void controls the remaining mass. Maintain readable title scale. |
| G03 | One edge under pressure | A voice confined by its frame | Push native letterforms into one edge so crop and counter-pressure carry the tension. Do not crop away required title glyphs. |
| G04 | Overprint as a third state | Two lives creating something neither owns | Two limited-color forms make the title at their intersection. Specify the exact overlapping regions, not random print misregistration. |
| G05 | Density cliff | Overload meeting silence | A compressed field stops sharply at the title's reading boundary, leaving a large quiet zone. Avoid uniform all-over detail. |
| G06 | One broken repetition | Refusal; departure from a social rule | One interruption in a strict flat rhythm makes the title legible or changes its path. No decorative grids or fake labels. |
| G07 | Elastic title gesture | A held breath; tension before release | Stretch one native stroke into a decisive curve while protecting recognisable glyph skeletons. Shape, not 3D material, carries force. |
| G08 | Folded reading plane | Two incompatible versions of one event | A single conceptual fold reverses which side is figure or ground. Make the title share that fold without a separate caption. |

## Events, traces, and systems

| ID | Mechanism | Meaning it can enact | Construct the difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E01 | One impossible ordinary event | A world quietly breaking its promise | An otherwise believable room, street, or gesture contains one impossible title-causing event. Do not add a stack of surreal props. |
| E02 | Tension before contact | Desire; a decision not yet made | Two forms almost meet, and the gap itself creates the exact title. The gap must remain visible at thumbnail scale. |
| E03 | Residue of a missing action | A person gone; an event only inferred | Condensation, dust displacement, sun fading, or a peeled surface leaves the title. Use a close view with one strong trace. |
| E04 | Exposed cross-section | What a polished exterior hides | A cut reveals native title structure within layers. The section, rather than a freestanding letter object, is the main image. |
| E05 | Choreographed collective action | Dependence; solidarity; coordinated pressure | A few figures' connected actions create one title relationship. Define one cause-and-effect scene, not a collage of poses. |
| E06 | Time displacement in one frame | Someone moving on while something stays | Preserve one stable title skeleton while one scene element has two time states. Keep the difference local and readable. |
| E07 | Interrupted transmission | A message that cannot reach its recipient | A title path changes at one failed connection; the failure has an observable consequence on the image. Avoid arbitrary sci-fi interfaces. |
| E08 | Almost-empty artifact | A ritual whose meaning outlives its object | A severe crop, imprint, opening, or missing component turns one release-specific artifact into the title structure. Avoid a product catalogue. |

## Illusion checks

Treat an illusion as a relationship to verify, not as a style keyword. Write
the viewing condition and the observation that would falsify it before
generation. Then inspect the actual result:

- **Figure/ground:** can both intended readings be traced from the same
  contour, rather than merely described in the prompt?
- **Anamorphosis:** do fragments align in the final fixed view, with correct
  spelling and no contradictory second title? A still cover cannot require the
  viewer to physically move around the image.
- **Impossible geometry:** is the impossible join intentional and spatially
  specific, rather than a generic malformed object? Does it survive reduction?
- **Shadow/reflection:** do object, light, occluder, reflecting plane, and
  letter orientation support the claimed event? Inspect mirrored text.
- **Near/far:** does reduction reveal the intended title and preserve its
  reading, rather than produce mush? Use actual 56/128/256 px previews.

If a mechanism does not work, simplify the relationship, change the camera,
or replace that concept. More detail cannot repair a false optical relation.
Do not advertise a physically correct ambigram, stereogram, or impossible
object merely because a model generated an attractive approximation.

## Evidence boundary

These recipes extend the skill's design vocabulary. They have not all been
benchmarked on GPT Image 2.6. Keep trial results scoped to the actual model,
host, prompt, title complexity, and reference use. Promote a recipe into a
verified construction rule only through `references/production-learning.md`.
Use official model documentation for API capabilities; a visual concept is
not a new API feature.
