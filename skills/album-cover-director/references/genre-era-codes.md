# Genre and era codes

Genre and era are independent axes. Use them to create recognition and tension, not to choose the primary pattern automatically.

## Genre anchor / betrayal method

For each direction:

1. name one audience-recognizable anchor in typography, palette, image source, density, gesture, or material;
2. name one expected code to remove, invert, or replace;
3. state why the betrayal clarifies the track rather than merely appearing novel.

Examples:

- jazz: retain improvisational cropping; betray archival black-and-white with a synthetic color field;
- metal: retain mass and threat; betray dark illustration with clinical white space;
- ambient: retain spacious pacing; betray soft blur with sharply indexed objects;
- dance: retain pulse and repetition; betray neon nightlife with daylight domestic material;
- singer-songwriter: retain intimacy; betray literal portraiture with a worn personal object;
- hip-hop: retain declarative identity; betray centered portraiture with diagrammatic evidence;
- city pop: retain graphic polish; betray nostalgic sunset warmth with cold fluorescent absence.

## Translate sound before genre

Record audible evidence, then convert it to visual behavior:

| Audible trait | Possible visual behavior |
| --- | --- |
| sparse arrangement | fewer elements, larger intervals, exposed edges |
| dense layering | overlap, compression, nested depth, but one dominant signal |
| sharp transient | hard edge, cut, flash, short repeated interval |
| soft attack | feathered boundary, gradual reveal, suspended material |
| unstable pitch or tape drift | warped baseline, repeated offset, physical deformation |
| dry close vocal | shallow space, direct crop, tactile surface |
| distant reverberant vocal | deep field, occlusion, architectural scale |
| syncopation | irregular spacing with a controlled repeating rule |
| long drone | continuous field, horizon, slow gradient, sustained material process |
| abrupt section change | split system, hinge, fold, collision, or purposeful anomaly |

Use at most three translated behaviors in one direction. The table generates options; it does not prescribe an aesthetic.

## Era as production behavior

Avoid decade costume. Translate an era into production constraints:

- 1940s–1950s: limited spot color, photographic halftone, hand-set display hierarchy, physical sleeve wear;
- 1960s–1970s: expanded studio photography, illustration, collage, psychedelia, corporate modernism, gatefold thinking;
- 1980s–1990s: airbrush, photocopy, desktop publishing, rave flyers, early 3D, sampling, deliberate anti-design;
- 2000s–2014: compact-disc detail, web-native gloss, vector systems, lo-fi digital cameras, post-internet collage;
- 2015–present: streaming-thumbnail compression, direct image economy, phone-camera intimacy, 3D/material simulation, identity systems across motion and social crops.

Use one or two behaviors at most. Do not add fake aging, VHS noise, or halftone merely to announce a decade.

## Anti-convergence checks

Across a series, compare subject placement, palette family, setting, title zone, camera distance, pattern, material, and dominant geometry. If three consecutive covers repeat four or more of these, force the next direction to change the organizing pattern and at least two additional axes.
